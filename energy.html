Re-framing our relationship
with energy

This project explores how design can help households navigate new energy pricing models, including power-based tariffs and real-time feedback. Developed in collaboration with KTH, Uppsala University, Ellevio, Bright, and funded by the Swedish Energy Agency.

Rather than treating energy flexibility as a purely technical issue, the work reframes it as a question of design and perception — how people understand energy, cost and climate impact in everyday life.

Through interviews, workshops and usability testing with consumers and prosumers, I developed and tested concepts for real-time feedback, spanning digital interfaces and ambient, physical visualisations.

DESIGN PROCESS

My contribution focused on user research, facilitating workshops and concept development - helping translate energy systems into solutions that considers everyday life contexts - rather than fighting them. Some of the insights from the research were:

  • Households are primarily motivated by economic effects, while climate impact is often perceived as a positive 'side effect'.
  • Broken down real-time feedback (showing how specific activities affect usage) has the strongest effects on behaviour.
  • The distinction between energy (kWh) and power (kW) is poorly understood and requires careful design to avoid misinterpretation.
  • Comparisons work best when based on a household’s own historical data, rather than comparisons with other households.
  • Feedback must be personal, coordinated and actionable to be effective.



Based on these insights, I developed concepts to meet the needs and behaviours, this resulted in prototypes that were tested on the user groups.
